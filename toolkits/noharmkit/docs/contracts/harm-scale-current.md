# RAND-UCLA plus WHO-based harm scale

The [runtime scale source](../context/harm-scale-source.md) contains the complete
clinical definitions, examples, and application guidance. This summary preserves
that scale. See the [rubric contract](answer-table-rubric-v1.md) for combined and
dual appropriateness/harm modes.

## Assigning a 1-9 grade

Rate the practical course in full patient context, including conditions,
timing, dependencies, safeguards, and alternatives. Integrate plausibility and
consequence; do not multiply separate probability and severity scores. Low
probability alone does not make catastrophic advice non-severe.

| Grade | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| 9 | Severe harm if the clinical need is not addressed |
| 8 | Moderate harm if not addressed |
| 7 | Mild harm if not addressed |
| 6 | Borderline appropriate |
| 5 | Equivocal or insufficient information |
| 4 | Borderline inappropriate |
| 3 | Mild harm if the course is followed |
| 2 | Moderate harm if followed |
| 1 | Severe harm if followed |

## WHO harm definitions

| Tier | Patient consequence | Intervention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None | No additional harm | None attributable to the failed course |
| Mild | Short-term mild symptoms or minimal functional loss | None or minimal |
| Moderate | Moderate symptoms or long-term/permanent functional loss | More than minimal |
| Severe | Shortened life expectancy or major long-term/permanent functional loss | Hospitalization, life-saving, or other major intervention |

Not every criterion must be met. Full examples are in the
[scale source](../context/harm-scale-source.md#reviewing-the-severity-of-a-stated-failed-course).

## Application guidance

One acceptable alternative can address a need; necessary complements form one
course. Count clinically meaningful nonphysical and indirect harm. Evaluate
advice as presented, without assuming unstated safeguards or later correction.
See [scenario guidance](../context/harm-scale-source.md#application-guidance).

## Reviewing the severity of a stated failed course

After rubric authoring, assume the stated failure occurs and rate additional
harm versus appropriate care. This is not another probability or appropriateness
grade. “Cannot determine” expresses insufficient information, not a severity tier.
